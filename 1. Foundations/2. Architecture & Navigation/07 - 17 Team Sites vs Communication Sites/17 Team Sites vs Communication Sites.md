# Team Sites vs Communication Sites

Canonical documentation for Team Sites vs Communication Sites. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

The purpose of this documentation is to provide a clear understanding of the differences between Team Sites and Communication Sites, two types of sites used in collaborative environments. This topic exists to address the problem space of site selection and configuration, where users often struggle to determine which type of site best suits their needs. By understanding the characteristics, use cases, and best practices for each type of site, users can make informed decisions and create effective online spaces for collaboration and communication.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

## Definitions

The following definitions are used throughout this documentation:

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Team Site | A collaborative site designed for teams to work together on projects, share information, and manage content. |
| Communication Site | A site designed for broadcasting information to a wide audience, often used for company-wide announcements, news, and knowledge sharing. |
| Site Collection | A group of related sites that share common settings, security, and content. |
| Site Template | A pre-configured site design that provides a starting point for creating a new site. |

> [!TIP]
> Definitions should be stable over time; avoid contextual language.

## Core Concepts

The core concepts that make up this topic are:

### Team Sites
Team Sites are designed for collaborative work and are typically used by teams to manage projects, share documents, and track progress. They often include features such as document libraries, calendars, and task lists.

### Communication Sites
Communication Sites are designed for broadcasting information to a wide audience and are often used for company-wide announcements, news, and knowledge sharing. They typically include features such as news articles, blogs, and image galleries.

## Standard Model

The standard model for Team Sites and Communication Sites is to use a site collection with separate sites for each type. This allows for clear separation of content and functionality, making it easier to manage and maintain the sites.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Deviations from the standard model should be explicitly documented and justified.

## Common Patterns

Common patterns associated with Team Sites and Communication Sites include:

* Using a Team Site as a central hub for project management and collaboration
* Using a Communication Site as a company-wide news and announcement platform
* Creating separate sites for different departments or teams
* Using site templates to streamline site creation and configuration

## Anti-Patterns

Common mistakes or discouraged practices include:

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues.

* Using a single site for both team collaboration and company-wide communication
* Creating too many sites, leading to site sprawl and content duplication
* Not properly configuring site security and permissions
* Not regularly updating and maintaining site content

## Edge Cases

Edge cases related to Team Sites and Communication Sites include:

> [!CAUTION]
> Edge cases are frequently overlooked and may cause incorrect assumptions.

* Sites that require both collaborative and broadcasting functionality
* Sites that need to be accessible to external users or partners
* Sites that require custom branding or design
* Sites that need to integrate with other tools or systems
