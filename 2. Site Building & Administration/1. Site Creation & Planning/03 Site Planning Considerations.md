# Site Planning Considerations

Canonical documentation for Site Planning Considerations. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

Describe why this topic exists and what problem space it addresses. This section should be descriptive, not instructional.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

Site planning considerations exist to address the complexities of designing and developing sites that meet the needs of various stakeholders, including users, owners, and the environment. The problem space it addresses includes the need for efficient use of land, minimization of environmental impact, and creation of functional and aesthetically pleasing spaces. Effective site planning considers factors such as climate, topography, zoning regulations, and community needs to create sustainable and thriving sites.

## Core Concepts

Explain the fundamental ideas that make up the topic.

### Site Analysis
Site analysis involves assessing the physical and environmental characteristics of a site, including its topography, climate, soil conditions, and existing infrastructure. This analysis informs the site planning process and helps identify potential opportunities and constraints.

### Land Use Planning
Land use planning involves determining the most appropriate use of a site based on its characteristics, zoning regulations, and community needs. This includes considering factors such as density, mixed-use development, and transportation options.

## Common Patterns

Document recurring patterns or approaches associated with this topic.

* Mixed-use development: combining residential, commercial, and recreational uses in a single site
* Green infrastructure: incorporating natural systems and processes into site design to manage stormwater, improve air quality, and enhance biodiversity
* Transit-oriented development: designing sites around public transportation hubs to reduce reliance on personal vehicles and promote walkability

## Anti-Patterns

Describe common mistakes or discouraged practices.

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues.

* Ignoring environmental impact: failing to consider the potential environmental consequences of site development, such as soil erosion or water pollution
* Over-reliance on personal vehicles: designing sites that prioritize car use over pedestrian-friendly and public transportation options
* Inadequate community engagement: failing to involve stakeholders and community members in the site planning process, leading to unmet needs and unrealized opportunities
