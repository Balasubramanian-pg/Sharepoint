# Communication Site Designs

Canonical documentation for Communication Site Designs. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

Communication Site Designs refer to the planning, creation, and implementation of digital platforms used for disseminating information, facilitating collaboration, and engaging with audiences. The purpose of this topic is to provide a comprehensive framework for designing effective communication sites, addressing the problem space of information dissemination, collaboration, and audience engagement in various contexts, including corporate, educational, and community settings. This documentation aims to establish a common understanding of the principles, concepts, and best practices involved in designing communication sites, thereby facilitating the creation of well-structured, user-friendly, and efficient digital platforms.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

## Scope

The scope of this topic includes the concepts, principles, and best practices related to the design of communication sites. The following aspects are in scope:

**In scope:**
* Information architecture and taxonomy
* User experience (UX) and user interface (UI) design
* Content strategy and creation
* Collaboration and engagement features

**Out of scope:**
* Tool-specific implementations (e.g., SharePoint, WordPress, or Drupal)
* Vendor-specific behavior and proprietary technologies
* Technical infrastructure and hosting considerations

## Core Concepts

The core concepts of Communication Site Designs include:

### Information Architecture
Information architecture refers to the organization and structure of content and functionality within a communication site. A well-designed information architecture enables users to easily find and access relevant information, facilitating effective communication and collaboration.

### User Experience (UX)
User experience (UX) encompasses the overall experience and satisfaction of users interacting with a communication site. A positive UX is critical to engaging audiences, promoting collaboration, and achieving the site's intended purpose.

### Content Strategy
Content strategy involves the planning, creation, and management of content within a communication site. A effective content strategy ensures that content is relevant, accurate, and up-to-date, supporting the site's goals and objectives.

## Standard Model

The standard model for Communication Site Designs involves the following components:

1. **Information Architecture**: A clear and consistent information architecture that organizes content and functionality.
2. **User Experience (UX)**: A user-centered design approach that prioritizes usability, accessibility, and engagement.
3. **Content Strategy**: A well-planned content strategy that ensures relevant, accurate, and up-to-date content.
4. **Collaboration and Engagement Features**: Features that facilitate collaboration, feedback, and engagement among users.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Deviations from the standard model should be explicitly documented and justified.

## Common Patterns

Common patterns associated with Communication Site Designs include:

* **Hub-and-Spoke Model**: A central hub that connects to related spokes, facilitating navigation and content discovery.
* **Card-Based Design**: A design approach that uses cards or tiles to present content, promoting scannability and usability.
* **Responsive Design**: A design approach that adapts to different screen sizes and devices, ensuring accessibility and usability.

## Anti-Patterns

Common mistakes or discouraged practices in Communication Site Designs include:

* **Information Overload**: Presenting too much information, leading to user confusion and decreased engagement.
* **Poor Navigation**: Failing to provide clear and consistent navigation, making it difficult for users to find relevant content.
* **Outdated Content**: Failing to update content regularly, leading to decreased credibility and user trust.

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues.

## Edge Cases

Edge cases related to Communication Site Designs include:

* **Multilingual Support**: Supporting multiple languages and character sets, requiring consideration of font, layout, and translation issues.
* **Accessibility Requirements**: Ensuring that the site is accessible to users with disabilities, requiring consideration of WCAG guidelines and assistive technologies.
* **High-Traffic Scenarios**: Handling high traffic volumes, requiring consideration of scalability, performance, and caching strategies.

> [!CAUTION]
> Edge cases are frequently overlooked and may cause incorrect assumptions.
