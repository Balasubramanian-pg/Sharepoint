# Creating Team Sites Step by Step

Canonical documentation for Creating Team Sites Step by Step. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

The purpose of this documentation is to provide a comprehensive guide for creating team sites, addressing the need for a structured approach to collaborative workspace setup. This topic exists to help teams and organizations establish effective online environments that facilitate communication, productivity, and knowledge sharing. By following the steps and guidelines outlined in this document, teams can create tailored sites that meet their specific needs, enhancing overall collaboration and project management. The problem space this topic addresses includes the lack of standardization in team site creation, leading to inconsistencies, inefficiencies, and difficulties in scaling collaborative efforts.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

## Scope

The scope of this topic includes the fundamental concepts, best practices, and standard models for creating team sites. It covers the essential steps and considerations for planning, designing, and implementing team sites, ensuring they are functional, user-friendly, and aligned with organizational goals.

**In scope:**
* Team site planning and design
* Site structure and organization
* Content management and governance
* User roles and permissions
* Integration with other collaboration tools

**Out of scope:**
* Tool-specific implementations (e.g., SharePoint, Google Workspace)
* Vendor-specific behavior and customizations
* Advanced development or coding for team sites
* IT infrastructure and network configurations

## Core Concepts

The core concepts of creating team sites step by step include:

### Team Site Planning
Team site planning involves defining the purpose, scope, and requirements of the site. This includes identifying the target audience, determining the necessary features and functionalities, and establishing clear goals and objectives for the site.

### Site Design and Structure
The design and structure of a team site are critical for usability and effectiveness. This includes organizing content in a logical and accessible manner, designing an intuitive navigation system, and ensuring the site's layout is visually appealing and consistent with the organization's brand.

## Standard Model

The standard model for creating team sites involves the following steps:
1. **Planning and Definition**: Define the site's purpose, identify the target audience, and determine the necessary features and functionalities.
2. **Design and Structure**: Design the site's layout, organize content, and establish a navigation system.
3. **Content Creation and Management**: Develop a content strategy, create initial content, and establish governance policies.
4. **Configuration and Customization**: Configure the site's settings, customize the layout and design as needed, and integrate with other collaboration tools.
5. **Testing and Launch**: Test the site for usability and functionality, make any necessary adjustments, and launch the site.
6. **Maintenance and Updates**: Regularly update content, monitor site performance, and make improvements as needed.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Deviations from the standard model should be explicitly documented and justified.

## Common Patterns

Common patterns associated with creating team sites include:
* Using templates to streamline the site creation process
* Implementing a consistent information architecture across multiple team sites
* Integrating team sites with other collaboration tools and platforms
* Establishing clear roles and responsibilities for site management and maintenance

## Anti-Patterns

Anti-patterns to avoid when creating team sites include:
* **Insufficient Planning**: Failing to define the site's purpose, target audience, and necessary features, leading to a site that does not meet the team's needs.
* **Poor Content Management**: Allowing content to become outdated, disorganized, or inaccessible, which can lead to user frustration and decreased productivity.
* **Inadequate Security and Permissions**: Failing to establish appropriate user roles and permissions, potentially exposing sensitive information or allowing unauthorized access.

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues.

## Edge Cases

Edge cases to consider when creating team sites include:
* **Multi-Language Support**: Accommodating teams with members who speak different languages, requiring translation services or multilingual content support.
* **External Collaboration**: Collaborating with external partners or vendors, necessitating secure guest access and controlled information sharing.
* **Compliance and Regulatory Requirements**: Ensuring team sites comply with organizational, legal, or regulatory requirements, such as data protection and privacy laws.

> [!CAUTION]
> Edge cases are frequently overlooked and may cause incorrect assumptions.
