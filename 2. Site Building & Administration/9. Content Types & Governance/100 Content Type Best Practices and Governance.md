# Content Type Best Practices and Governance

Canonical documentation for Content Type Best Practices and Governance. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

The purpose of Content Type Best Practices and Governance is to provide a framework for creating, managing, and maintaining high-quality content across various channels and platforms. This topic exists to address the problem of inconsistent, inaccurate, or incomplete content, which can lead to confusion, mistrust, or disengagement among audiences. By establishing best practices and governance guidelines, organizations can ensure that their content is consistent, relevant, and effective in achieving its intended goals. This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative, providing a foundation for content creators, managers, and stakeholders to develop and implement content strategies that meet their specific needs.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

## Core Concepts

The following core concepts are fundamental to Content Type Best Practices and Governance:

### Content Strategy
A content strategy is a plan for creating, managing, and maintaining content to achieve specific business goals or objectives. It involves understanding the target audience, defining the content's purpose and scope, and establishing a framework for content creation, review, and maintenance.

### Content Governance
Content governance refers to the set of policies, procedures, and standards that regulate the creation, management, and maintenance of content within an organization. It involves establishing clear roles and responsibilities, defining content workflows and approval processes, and ensuring compliance with regulatory requirements or industry standards.

## Standard Model

The standard model for Content Type Best Practices and Governance involves the following components:

1. **Content Strategy**: Develop a content strategy that aligns with business goals and objectives.
2. **Content Creation**: Create high-quality content that meets the needs of the target audience.
3. **Content Review and Approval**: Establish a review and approval process to ensure content meets quality and compliance standards.
4. **Content Maintenance**: Regularly update and maintain content to ensure accuracy, relevance, and consistency.
5. **Content Governance**: Establish a governance framework to regulate content creation, management, and maintenance.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Deviations from the standard model should be explicitly documented and justified.

## Common Patterns

The following common patterns are associated with Content Type Best Practices and Governance:

* **Content Templating**: Using templates to standardize content formats and structures.
* **Content Reuse**: Reusing content across multiple channels or platforms to reduce duplication and improve efficiency.
* **Content Localization**: Adapting content for different languages, cultures, or regions to improve relevance and engagement.

## Anti-Patterns

The following anti-patterns are common mistakes or discouraged practices in Content Type Best Practices and Governance:

* **Content Duplication**: Creating duplicate content across multiple channels or platforms, leading to inconsistencies and maintenance issues.
* **Content Chaos**: Failing to establish clear governance, leading to unmanaged content creation, inconsistent quality, and compliance risks.
* **Content Neglect**: Failing to regularly update and maintain content, leading to outdated, inaccurate, or irrelevant information.

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues.

## Edge Cases

The following edge cases are unusual, ambiguous, or boundary scenarios related to Content Type Best Practices and Governance:

* **Content Ownership**: Determining ownership and responsibility for content created by multiple stakeholders or teams.
* **Content Classification**: Classifying content into specific categories or formats, such as sensitive or confidential information.
* **Content Archiving**: Deciding when and how to archive or retire content, balancing preservation and maintenance costs.

> [!CAUTION]
> Edge cases are frequently overlooked and may cause incorrect assumptions.
