# Crisis Management Template

Canonical documentation for Crisis Management Template. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

The Crisis Management Template exists to provide a structured approach to managing and responding to crises, ensuring that organizations can mitigate the impact of unexpected events and minimize disruption to their operations. This template addresses the problem space of crisis management by providing a framework for identifying, assessing, and responding to crises, as well as for reviewing and improving crisis management processes. The goal of this template is to enable organizations to develop a comprehensive crisis management plan that can be tailored to their specific needs and circumstances.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

## Scope

**In scope:**
* Crisis management planning and processes
* Crisis response and recovery strategies
* Communication and stakeholder management during a crisis
* Review and improvement of crisis management processes

**Out of scope:**
* Tool-specific implementations (e.g., crisis management software)
* Vendor-specific behavior (e.g., crisis management services provided by external vendors)
* Business continuity planning (although crisis management and business continuity planning are related, they are distinct topics)

## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Crisis | An unexpected event or situation that has the potential to cause significant harm to an organization, its stakeholders, or the environment. |
| Crisis Management | The process of identifying, assessing, and responding to crises, as well as reviewing and improving crisis management processes. |
| Crisis Management Plan | A document that outlines the procedures and protocols for managing and responding to crises. |
| Stakeholder | An individual or group that has an interest or concern in the organization, such as employees, customers, suppliers, or the general public. |

> [!TIP]
> Definitions should be stable over time; avoid contextual language.

## Core Concepts

### Crisis Management Lifecycle
The crisis management lifecycle refers to the ongoing process of identifying, assessing, and responding to crises, as well as reviewing and improving crisis management processes. This lifecycle is continuous and iterative, with each stage informing and influencing the others.

### Crisis Classification
Crisis classification refers to the process of categorizing crises based on their type, severity, and impact. This classification helps organizations to prioritize their response efforts and allocate resources effectively.

## Standard Model

The standard model for crisis management involves the following stages:
1. **Crisis Identification**: Identifying potential crises and assessing their likelihood and potential impact.
2. **Crisis Assessment**: Assessing the crisis and determining its severity and impact.
3. **Crisis Response**: Responding to the crisis, including activating crisis management plans and protocols.
4. **Crisis Recovery**: Recovering from the crisis, including restoring operations and services.
5. **Crisis Review**: Reviewing the crisis management process and identifying areas for improvement.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Deviations from the standard model should be explicitly documented and justified.

## Common Patterns

* **Crisis Management Team**: Establishing a dedicated team to manage and respond to crises.
* **Crisis Communication Plan**: Developing a plan for communicating with stakeholders during a crisis.
* **Crisis Training and Exercises**: Conducting regular training and exercises to ensure that crisis management teams are prepared and effective.

## Anti-Patterns

* **Lack of Planning**: Failing to develop a crisis management plan or failing to review and update the plan regularly.
* **Inadequate Communication**: Failing to communicate effectively with stakeholders during a crisis, leading to confusion and mistrust.
* **Insufficient Resources**: Failing to allocate sufficient resources to support crisis management efforts, leading to inadequate response and recovery.

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues, and can exacerbate the impact of a crisis.

## Edge Cases

* **Multiple Crises**: Managing multiple crises simultaneously, which can require significant resources and coordination.
* **Uncertain or Evolving Crises**: Managing crises that are uncertain or evolving, which can require flexible and adaptive response strategies.
* **Crisis Fatigue**: Managing the psychological and emotional impact of repeated or prolonged crises on crisis management teams and stakeholders.

> [!CAUTION]
> Edge cases are frequently overlooked and may cause incorrect assumptions.

## Related Topics

* **Business Continuity Planning**: Developing plans to ensure the continuity of business operations during and after a crisis.
* **Risk Management**: Identifying, assessing, and mitigating risks that could lead to crises.
* **Emergency Management**: Managing and responding to emergencies, such as natural disasters or accidents.
