# Communication Site Topic Design

Canonical documentation for Communication Site Topic Design. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

The Communication Site Topic Design exists to provide a structured approach to creating and managing communication sites, addressing the problem space of information dissemination, collaboration, and knowledge sharing within organizations. Effective communication site design is crucial for facilitating seamless information exchange, promoting transparency, and fostering a sense of community among stakeholders. This topic aims to establish a common understanding of the principles, concepts, and best practices involved in designing and implementing communication sites, thereby enhancing the overall user experience and organizational efficiency.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

## Scope

The scope of Communication Site Topic Design encompasses the fundamental principles, concepts, and guidelines for designing and managing communication sites. The following aspects are included within the scope:

**In scope:**
* Information architecture and taxonomy
* User experience (UX) and user interface (UI) design
* Content strategy and governance
* Collaboration and community engagement

**Out of scope:**
* Tool-specific implementations (e.g., SharePoint, WordPress)
* Vendor-specific behavior and customizations
* Technical infrastructure and hosting considerations

## Definitions

The following terms are defined to provide clarity and consistency throughout this documentation:

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Communication Site | A digital platform designed to facilitate information sharing, collaboration, and community engagement among stakeholders. |
| Information Architecture | The structural design of information and content within a communication site, including taxonomy, navigation, and search functionality. |
| Content Strategy | A planned approach to creating, publishing, and managing content across a communication site, aligned with organizational goals and audience needs. |

> [!TIP]
> Definitions should be stable over time; avoid contextual language.

## Core Concepts

The Communication Site Topic Design is built upon the following fundamental concepts:

### Information Hierarchy
A clear and logical structure for organizing and presenting information, enabling users to easily navigate and find relevant content.

### Content Governance
A set of policies, procedures, and standards for managing content across a communication site, ensuring consistency, accuracy, and compliance with organizational guidelines.

### User Engagement
The design and implementation of features and functionalities that promote user participation, feedback, and community building within a communication site.

## Standard Model

The standard model for Communication Site Topic Design involves a user-centered approach, incorporating the following elements:

1. **Information Architecture**: A well-structured and intuitive information hierarchy.
2. **Content Strategy**: A planned and consistent approach to content creation and management.
3. **User Experience (UX) Design**: An intuitive and engaging user interface, aligned with organizational branding and user needs.
4. **Collaboration and Community Features**: Integrated tools and functionalities that facilitate user engagement and community building.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Deviations from the standard model should be explicitly documented and justified.

## Common Patterns

The following patterns are commonly observed in effective communication site designs:

* **Hub-and-Spoke Model**: A central hub for key information and resources, with linked spokes for related content and sub-sites.
* **Card-Based Design**: A visual design approach using cards or tiles to present information in a concise and easily digestible format.

## Anti-Patterns

The following anti-patterns are commonly encountered in communication site design and should be avoided:

* **Information Overload**: Presenting too much information in a single location, leading to user confusion and decreased engagement.
* **Insufficient Navigation**: Failing to provide clear and consistent navigation, making it difficult for users to find relevant information.

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues.

## Edge Cases

The following edge cases should be considered when designing a communication site:

* **Multilingual Support**: Accommodating users who speak different languages, requiring translation and localization of content.
* **Accessibility Requirements**: Ensuring that the communication site is accessible to users with disabilities, in compliance with relevant laws and regulations.

> [!CAUTION]
> Edge cases are frequently overlooked and may cause incorrect assumptions.
