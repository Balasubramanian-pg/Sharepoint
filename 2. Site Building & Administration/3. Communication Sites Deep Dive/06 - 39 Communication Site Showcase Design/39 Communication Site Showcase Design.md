# Communication Site Showcase Design

Canonical documentation for Communication Site Showcase Design. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

The Communication Site Showcase Design topic exists to provide a framework for creating effective and engaging communication sites that showcase an organization's content, news, and information. This problem space addresses the need for a well-structured and visually appealing platform to communicate with various stakeholders, including employees, customers, and partners. The goal of a communication site showcase design is to provide a centralized location for information sharing, collaboration, and community building. By establishing a clear and consistent design approach, organizations can ensure that their communication sites are easy to navigate, accessible, and aligned with their brand identity.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

## Core Concepts

The following core concepts make up the foundation of a communication site showcase design:

### Concept One: Content Strategy
A well-planned content strategy is essential for a successful communication site showcase design. This involves defining the types of content to be published, the target audience, and the channels for content distribution.

### Concept Two: User Experience (UX) Design
A user-centered design approach is critical for creating an engaging and accessible communication site showcase. This involves understanding user needs, behaviors, and motivations to design an intuitive and seamless user experience.

## Standard Model

The standard model for a communication site showcase design consists of the following components:

1. **Header and Navigation**: A consistent header and navigation system to facilitate easy access to main sections and content.
2. **Hero Section**: A prominent section showcasing key messages, news, or featured content.
3. **Content Sections**: Organized sections for different types of content, such as news, articles, or resources.
4. **Call-to-Actions (CTAs)**: Clear and prominent CTAs to encourage user engagement and conversion.
5. **Footer and Secondary Navigation**: A footer section with secondary navigation, contact information, and other relevant details.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Deviations from the standard model should be explicitly documented and justified.

## Common Patterns

The following patterns are commonly associated with communication site showcase design:

* **Card-based layout**: Using cards or tiles to display content in a visually appealing and easy-to-consume format.
* **Hero image or video**: Using a high-quality image or video to create a visually striking hero section.
* **Accordion or tabbed content**: Using accordions or tabs to organize and hide/show content to reduce clutter and improve usability.

## Anti-Patterns

The following anti-patterns are common mistakes or discouraged practices in communication site showcase design:

* **Information overload**: Overwhelming users with too much content, leading to cognitive overload and decreased engagement.
* **Poor navigation and information architecture**: Failing to provide a clear and consistent navigation system, making it difficult for users to find content.
* **Inconsistent branding and visual design**: Failing to maintain a consistent visual design and branding throughout the site, leading to a disjointed user experience.

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues.

## Edge Cases

The following edge cases are unusual, ambiguous, or boundary scenarios related to communication site showcase design:

* **Multilingual support**: Designing a communication site showcase to support multiple languages and regional preferences.
* **Accessibility for users with disabilities**: Ensuring that the site is accessible and usable for users with disabilities, such as visual or hearing impairments.
* **Integration with other systems or tools**: Integrating the communication site showcase with other systems or tools, such as CRM or marketing automation platforms.

> [!CAUTION]
> Edge cases are frequently overlooked and may cause incorrect assumptions.
