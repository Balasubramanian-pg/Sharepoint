# Site Designs Technical Overview

Canonical documentation for Site Designs Technical Overview. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

The Site Designs Technical Overview topic exists to provide a comprehensive understanding of the technical aspects of site design, addressing the problem space of creating and managing complex digital environments. This includes the planning, development, and maintenance of websites, web applications, and other digital platforms. The goal of this topic is to establish a common language and framework for discussing site design, enabling effective communication and collaboration among stakeholders, including designers, developers, and content creators.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

## Scope

The scope of this topic includes the technical aspects of site design, focusing on the underlying principles, concepts, and best practices that apply to various digital platforms.

**In scope:**
* Site architecture and information architecture
* User experience (UX) and user interface (UI) design
* Front-end development and coding standards
* Content management systems (CMS) and content strategy

**Out of scope:**
* Tool-specific implementations (e.g., WordPress, Drupal, or Joomla)
* Vendor-specific behavior (e.g., proprietary CMS or software)
* Aesthetic design principles (e.g., visual design, branding, or graphic design)

## Definitions

The following terms are used throughout this documentation:

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Site | A collection of related web pages, including the home page, hosted on a single domain or subdomain. |
| Design | The planning, creation, and implementation of the visual and functional aspects of a site. |
| Information Architecture | The organization and structure of content and functionality within a site. |
| User Experience (UX) | The overall experience and satisfaction of a user when interacting with a site. |
| Front-end Development | The process of building the client-side of a site, using programming languages like HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. |

> [!TIP]
> Definitions should be stable over time; avoid contextual language.

## Core Concepts

The following core concepts form the foundation of site design:

### Concept One: Site Architecture
Site architecture refers to the overall structure and organization of a site, including the relationships between pages, sections, and content. A well-designed site architecture enables users to navigate and find information efficiently.

### Concept Two: User-Centered Design
User-centered design is an approach to site design that prioritizes the needs, goals, and behaviors of the target audience. This involves creating personas, user journeys, and wireframes to ensure that the site is intuitive, accessible, and engaging.

## Standard Model

The standard model for site design involves the following components:

1. **Discovery**: Research and planning phase, including stakeholder interviews, user research, and competitive analysis.
2. **Design**: Creation of visual and functional design concepts, including wireframes, prototypes, and high-fidelity designs.
3. **Development**: Front-end and back-end development, including coding, testing, and deployment.
4. **Launch**: Deployment of the site, including setup, configuration, and testing.
5. **Maintenance**: Ongoing updates, bug fixes, and performance optimization.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Deviations from the standard model should be explicitly documented and justified.

## Common Patterns

The following patterns are commonly associated with site design:

* **Responsive Design**: Designing sites to adapt to different screen sizes, devices, and orientations.
* **Mobile-First Design**: Prioritizing mobile devices and designing for smaller screens first.
* **Progressive Enhancement**: Enhancing the user experience by adding features and functionality gradually.

## Anti-Patterns

The following anti-patterns are common mistakes or discouraged practices in site design:

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues.

* **Over-Engineering**: Over-complicating the design or development process, leading to unnecessary complexity and technical debt.
* **Lack of Accessibility**: Failing to design and develop sites that are accessible to users with disabilities.
* **Poor Performance Optimization**: Neglecting to optimize site performance, leading to slow load times and poor user experience.

## Edge Cases

The following edge cases are unusual, ambiguous, or boundary scenarios related to site design:

> [!CAUTION]
> Edge cases are frequently overlooked and may cause incorrect assumptions.

* **Internationalization and Localization**: Designing and developing sites for multiple languages, cultures, and regions.
* **Accessibility for Users with Disabilities**: Designing and developing sites that are accessible to users with disabilities, including visual, auditory, motor, or cognitive disabilities.
* **Legacy System Integration**: Integrating new sites with legacy systems, including older technologies, databases, or infrastructure.
