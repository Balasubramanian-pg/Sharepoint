# Project Management Template

Canonical documentation for Project Management Template. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

The Project Management Template exists to provide a structured approach to managing projects, ensuring that all necessary aspects are considered and addressed. It aims to bridge the gap between project conception and execution, offering a standardized framework that can be adapted to various project types, sizes, and complexities. This template addresses the problem space of project management by providing a clear, step-by-step guide to planning, organizing, and controlling projects, thereby enhancing the likelihood of successful project outcomes.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

## Core Concepts

Explain the fundamental ideas that make up the topic.

### Project Life Cycle
The project life cycle refers to the series of phases that a project goes through, from initiation to closure. The typical phases include:
* Initiation: Defining the project's objectives, scope, and stakeholders.
* Planning: Developing a detailed project plan, including the scope statement, WBS, and schedule.
* Execution: Carrying out the project tasks and activities.
* Monitoring and Control: Tracking progress, identifying and addressing deviations from the plan.
* Closure: Formalizing the project's completion, documenting lessons learned, and evaluating the outcome.

### Project Management Processes
Project management processes refer to the set of activities, tools, and techniques used to manage a project. These processes include:
* Integration Management: Coordinating all aspects of the project.
* Scope Management: Defining and controlling the project's scope.
* Time Management: Developing and controlling the project schedule.
* Cost Management: Establishing and controlling the project budget.
* Quality Management: Ensuring the project meets the required standards and specifications.
* Resource Management: Allocating and managing the project's resources.
* Communications Management: Coordinating stakeholder engagement and information exchange.
* Risk Management: Identifying, assessing, and mitigating potential risks.
* Procurement Management: Acquiring goods and services from external sources.

## Standard Model

Describe the generally accepted or recommended model for this topic.

The standard model for project management templates typically includes the following components:
* Project Charter: A document that outlines the project's objectives, scope, and stakeholders.
* Project Management Plan: A comprehensive document that describes the project's approach, scope, schedule, budget, and resources.
* Work Breakdown Structure (WBS): A hierarchical decomposition of the project's scope into manageable tasks and activities.
* Gantt Chart: A type of bar chart that illustrates the project schedule, showing the relationships between tasks and activities.
* Resource Allocation Plan: A document that outlines the project's resource requirements and allocation strategy.
* Risk Management Plan: A document that identifies, assesses, and mitigates potential risks.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Deviations from the standard model should be explicitly documented and justified.

## Common Patterns

Document recurring patterns or approaches associated with this topic.

* Agile Project Management: An iterative and incremental approach to project management, emphasizing flexibility and adaptability.
* Waterfall Project Management: A linear and sequential approach to project management, emphasizing predictability and control.
* Hybrid Project Management: A combination of agile and waterfall approaches, blending the benefits of both.

## Anti-Patterns

Describe common mistakes or discouraged practices.

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues.

* Overly Complex Project Plans: Creating excessively detailed plans that are difficult to maintain and update.
* Insufficient Stakeholder Engagement: Failing to engage stakeholders effectively, leading to misunderstandings and misaligned expectations.
* Inadequate Risk Management: Ignoring or downplaying potential risks, resulting in unforeseen consequences and project delays.

## Edge Cases

Explain unusual, ambiguous, or boundary scenarios related to the topic.

> [!CAUTION]
> Edge cases are frequently overlooked and may cause incorrect assumptions.

* Multiple Project Dependencies: Managing projects with complex dependencies and interrelationships.
* Uncertain or Changing Requirements: Adapting to shifting project requirements or unclear stakeholder expectations.
* Limited Resources: Managing projects with constrained resources, such as budget, personnel, or equipment.
