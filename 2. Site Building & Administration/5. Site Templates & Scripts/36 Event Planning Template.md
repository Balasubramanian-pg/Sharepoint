# Event Planning Template

Canonical documentation for Event Planning Template. This document defines concepts, terminology, and standard usage.

## Purpose

The Event Planning Template exists to provide a structured approach to organizing and managing events, ensuring that all necessary aspects are considered and executed efficiently. This template addresses the problem space of event planning by offering a standardized framework for coordinating logistics, timelines, and resources. It helps event planners and organizers to streamline their processes, reduce errors, and enhance the overall quality of events. By using an Event Planning Template, individuals can better manage the complexities of event planning, from initial concept to post-event evaluation.

> [!NOTE]
> This documentation is intended to be implementation-agnostic and authoritative.

## Scope

**In scope:**
* Event concept and objective definition
* Venue and catering selection
* Budgeting and financial planning
* Scheduling and timeline management
* Marketing and promotion strategies
* Risk management and contingency planning

**Out of scope:**
* Tool-specific implementations (e.g., event management software)
* Vendor-specific behavior (e.g., catering or audio-visual services)
* Detailed financial accounting and tax implications

## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Event | A planned gathering of people, such as a conference, wedding, or festival, with a specific purpose or theme. |
| Venue | The physical location where an event takes place, including facilities, amenities, and services. |
| Stakeholder | An individual or group with a vested interest in the event's success, including attendees, sponsors, and organizers. |
| Timeline | A schedule of events and deadlines leading up to and during the event, including setup, execution, and teardown. |

> [!TIP]
> Definitions should be stable over time; avoid contextual language.

## Core Concepts

### Event Conceptualization
The process of defining the event's purpose, theme, and objectives, including identifying the target audience and desired outcomes.

### Event Logistics
The coordination of physical and technical aspects of the event, such as venue selection, catering, audio-visual equipment, and transportation.

## Standard Model

The standard model for Event Planning Templates involves the following stages:
1. **Event Conceptualization**: Define the event's purpose, theme, and objectives.
2. **Event Planning**: Develop a detailed plan, including budgeting, scheduling, and logistics.
3. **Event Execution**: Coordinate and manage the event, including setup, execution, and teardown.
4. **Event Evaluation**: Assess the event's success, gather feedback, and identify areas for improvement.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Deviations from the standard model should be explicitly documented and justified.

## Common Patterns

* **Agile Event Planning**: An iterative and flexible approach to event planning, emphasizing adaptability and responsiveness to changing circumstances.
* **Sustainable Event Planning**: A approach that prioritizes environmental and social responsibility, minimizing the event's ecological footprint and promoting positive social impacts.

## Anti-Patterns

* **Lack of Clear Objectives**: Failing to define clear event objectives, leading to confusion and misalignment among stakeholders.
* **Inadequate Risk Management**: Neglecting to identify and mitigate potential risks, resulting in unexpected problems and last-minute crises.

> [!WARNING]
> These anti-patterns often lead to maintenance or scalability issues, compromising the event's overall success.

## Edge Cases

* **Unforeseen Weather Conditions**: Inclement weather that affects outdoor events, requiring contingency planning and adaptability.
* **Last-Minute Cancellations**: Unexpected cancellations by speakers, performers, or vendors, necessitating rapid replacement or adjustment.

> [!CAUTION]
> Edge cases are frequently overlooked and may cause incorrect assumptions, so it's essential to anticipate and prepare for them.
